## 1. Decision

To mitigate hazards `H2-200` (Erratic High Value) and `H3-200` (Erratic Low Value), Safety Goal `SG-202` ("The software shall filter spurious pulses to ensure the integrity of the count") will be implemented through a combination of **software debouncing** and **rate plausibility checks**.

## 2. Mitigation Strategy Justification

Pulse readings from an electromechanical sensor in an electrically noisy environment (like the PiRacer) are susceptible to errors, primarily false pulses generated by noise or mechanical bouncing. A multi-layered mitigation approach is necessary.

### Implementation Strategy

1.  **Software Debouncing (Short-Term Filter):**
    *   **Description:** A software timer will be started on the rising (or falling) edge of each pulse. The software will ignore any subsequent interrupts on the same line for a short period (e.g., 1-2 milliseconds).
    *   **Justification:** This method effectively rejects the high-frequency, short-duration pulses characteristic of mechanical bouncing or high-frequency electrical noise, ensuring that a single mechanical event is counted as a single software pulse **[1]**.

2.  **Rate Plausibility Check (Medium-Term Filter):**
    *   **Description:** The software will keep track of the pulse rate (or calculated speed) over time. A new reading that implies a physically impossible acceleration or deceleration (e.g., from 10 km/h to 150 km/h in 100 milliseconds) will be considered invalid.
    *   **Justification:** This filter protects against bursts of noise that might pass the debouncing filter. By comparing the rate of change against the physical limits of the motor system, the software can reject readings that, while syntactically correct, are semantically implausible **[2]**.

## 3. Conclusion

The combination of software debouncing and rate plausibility checks provides a robust defense against spurious pulses, ensuring that only valid and physically possible readings are used by the control system, thereby mitigating hazards `H2-200` and `H3-200`.
